## 1.0.0: the canon formalization.md: price, overlay and levels (2026-11-01)

The system moves to the canon [docs/concept/formalization.en.md](docs/concept/formalization.en.md): the conditional forecast is built by the overlay measure $P_s$, the minimal Kullback–Leibler divergence among measures that keep the law of $R$ (the parameters and everything the causes do not name), the price of a scenario equals $\mathrm{dist}(s)=\mathbb E_P[-\log P(S\mid R)]$, and the system of models is simulated by recursion over levels $x^l=G^l(x^{<l},\omega^l)$. The model SDK version is 2.0.0, with no compatibility with old versions and files: the program had almost no users, and outdated versions are estimated anew.

- **Scenario price (was $-\log P(S)$).** A GHK sampler of the probability of the box of conditions in logarithms (`model_sdk/conditional.py`), the law of the conditions given $R$ with hidden coordinates (`MacroModel.constraint_law` → `ConstraintLaw`), the price core over the groups of $R$ (`model_sdk/dist.py`): the mean of $-\log P(S\mid R)$, $\log P(S)$, a standard error, Lemma 2 as `ContradictsModels`. A regime event costs $\mathbb E_P[-\log\pi_\theta(k,..,k)]$; impacts $O$ change the price (bounds shifted by $o^0$). An exact value is set by the field `value` and expands to $\pm\tau$, $\tau=0.1\sigma_q(1)$; the 1 % threshold applies only to intervals.
- **Overlay.** Hidden coordinates (the regime path of the MS-VAR, the volatility path of the BVAR) are chosen from 64 candidates by the probability their law gives the conditions; streams are separated ($[seed,r,0..3]$: parameters, hidden coordinate, free shocks, causes). Conditions on means and probabilities ($\ell\le\mathbb E_Q f\le r$, $Q(B)\ge p$) are a tilt of the causes $e^{\lambda'f}$ inside the groups of $R$ (a convex dual problem, `scenario/overlay.py`); weights reduce to equal ones by systematic resampling, and the report carries the effective size and the checks of the theorem.
- **Levels.** `Quantity.level`, and the registry rejects reading one's own level or higher; `UpperPaths` and `UpperHistory` in the contracts, the bundle simulates and estimates by level, the shocks of levels are independent; the reference second-level regression is the plugin `reflevel2`; conditions on the quantities of the levels above carry weights inside the groups of $R$ (zero survivors is Lemma 2), and the price decomposes by level (`Dist.by_level`). A model by a sample of paths: weights $1_S$, the price $-\log(\lvert S\rvert/n)$ (`model_sdk/overlay.py`), and the macro model template inherits it.
- **Scenario contract.** `causes` (shocks and the hidden coordinates `regime_path`/`volatility_path`), `regime_event` apart from a cause, `basis` on each element of $C$ and $O$, `approved_by`, `inner`, `Expectation`, `ProbabilityCondition`; `Severity` ($\delta$, $\pi_c$) is removed.
- **Report and metrics.** A run carries $s_0$ beside the forecast, the contributions of conditions and impacts on the same numbers (`contributions`), ES with an interval and 1 % trimming, the probability threshold and admission on paths, bases and the commit in `meta.json`. Backtest: two calculations per episode (without causes for $u$ and CRPS at $R=\theta$, with causes for sensitivity), the block `cause_sensitivity`; the rolling block: uniformity of $u$ at $s_0$ over all dates and a check of the price on an event family with a logarithmic score against the historical frequency and a block bootstrap. The reverse problem: `scenario/reverse.py`, `python -m mst.scenario reverse` and the action `reverse` of the tool `scenario`.

## 0.3.0: model checks on historical episodes, the portfolio tail and satellite shocks (2026-10-31)

`mst.metrics` checks models on history: on a past event the models roll back to a date before its start and predict what happened. The report brings the forecast and the fact together by each quantity and horizon month in a "level × horizon" table, separately by the tuning and validation halves, calculates the value of the scenario, passes a verdict by two standard errors and assesses the Acerbi–Székely tail of the portfolio. Satellite models receive the platform's shocks (a compatible extension of the model SDK contract).

- **`mst.metrics`: a check on historical episodes.**
  - CRPS is normalized by the quantity's typical move $\sigma_q(t)$ with a replacement chain: the nearest smaller horizon, then a larger one, then the median over the quantities of the level; the source and the number of pairs go into the cell row (`sigma_source`, `sigma_pairs`). The random walk calculates drift and noise by pairs at the exact distance on the grid, so a gap in a series no longer inflates them.
  - The aggregate is a "level × horizon" table: each quantity of a level weighs the same, however many cells it has. The table holds the model, the random walk, the base calculation and the naive forecast and two differences, from the random walk and from the base calculation; beside it is the split by the tuning and validation halves. `--target-level` selects the target level of the verdict and the value (`macro` by default).
  - The value of a scenario: the share of episodes where the forecast under the episode's scenario beats the base calculation (the model's unconditional forecast), and the mean difference; a split by the markup source `manual`/`llm`.
  - Intervals are told apart: the 95 % bootstrap of the **aggregate** over episodes (the verdict reads its standard error), a wild bootstrap with Webb weights over episode contributions with a divergence flag, and a Monte Carlo interval by a bootstrap over draws with `--mc-bootstrap`.
  - A verdict by two standard errors with `--against REPORT.json` on the validation half and on the same cells: a mismatch of the set is an error with a list, and cells without a normalized score are counted and named.
  - The Acerbi–Székely tail with `--portfolio ID`: $Z$, an interval by episodes, a wild interval, $Z$ with the 1 % most extreme draws trimmed, a horizon mark, dropped episodes by name. The realized loss is the change in portfolio value on the facts at the start and end of the episode window; $Z$ does not change admission.
  - Satellites: `outside_training_range` is calculated by the range of a node's **training sample**, recorded in the version part (`train_low`/`train_high`); a part without bounds is checked by the whole column and marked `legacy_training_range`.
- **Scenario implausibility.** The platform's $\mathrm{dist}(P_s,P)=-\log\mathrm P(C)$ on the unconditional calculation (`mst.model_sdk.dist`, the field `dist` in the scenario paths): a Monte Carlo share of paths, and at zero the exact box probability for a mixture of normals by the moments of $W\mathbf y$ of each parameter draw. In the calculation report it sits beside $\delta$; the meta records the definition and the estimation method. A scenario with no conditions has no distance.
- **`mst.model_sdk` 1.1.0.** `SatelliteModel.simulate` takes a keyword-only `shocks`, the standardized residuals of correlated drivers from the platform; with a bundle of two or more models the platform draws them itself by the full measured matrix, otherwise cross-model correlation does not reach the paths. Single-model bundles call `simulate` as before and reproduce bit for bit on the same seed. The new `mst.model_sdk.testing.check_shocks` catches a model ignoring the shocks.
- **Satellite cache.** The pointer is `satellites-<digest>`: the macro version name, the bytes of the driver catalog, their series on the calculation date, the configuration and versions of satellite plugins, the plugin SDK version and the assignments of quantities. An edit of the catalog, `spec.toml`, a series, a plugin version or an assignment gives a new key; old pointers by the macro version name are not read.
- **Episode plan.** In `episodes.toml`, `half` (`tuning`/`validation`, both halves non-empty) and `source` (`manual`/`llm`) are mandatory for each episode.

### Migration from 0.2.0

- **`episodes.toml`.** An episode without `half` or `source` will not load; halves are assigned in advance, by hand.
- **Satellite cache.** Pointers `satellites-<macro version name>` are not reused; the first calculation after the update estimates bundles anew (old version directories on disk do no harm).
- **Satellite versions.** Parts estimated before 0.3.0 are read (they have no training bounds), but their tail is marked `legacy_training_range`: a check by the whole column is weaker. For a strict check, re-estimate the satellites.
- **Multi-model bundles.** The platform now draws correlated shocks itself: the numbers of a multi-model bundle on the old seed will change (single-model ones will not). A plugin written for model SDK 1.0 keeps working in a single-model bundle; a multi-model one needs the parameter `shocks`.
- **Report.** `--details` has the new blocks `aggregate`, `halves`, `decision`, `tail`, `target_level` and `meta["missing"]` (empty: no unaccounted metrics); `aggregate["intervals"]` replaced `intervals["sampling_wild"]`, and wild intervals live inside the block `sampling`. `--portfolio` requires `--satellites`: positions are valued on driver paths, and without them the tail would drop entirely.

## 0.2.0: models as plugins (2026-10-31)

The repository is brought to the scheme of the stress-testing system: the choice of satellite model is generalized into a connected model per quantity, and the current models (BVAR and the hierarchical satellite) became ordinary plugins, connected on a par with those developed separately.

- **`mst.model_sdk`**: the contracts of macro and satellite models: `ScenarioPlan` (horizon, conditions $C$, driving shocks $D$), `AdditiveImpact` (a direct impact $o_l$ on any level), estimation contexts and the three model steps (`estimate` → version, `load` → the model of a version, `simulate` → paths). The conditional machinery (truncated normal, box Gibbs, Antolín-Díaz et al.) moved to `model_sdk.conditional` as common code.
- **Plugins of three kinds.** `Plugin` declares `models` (positions), `macro` and `satellites`; discovery covers the packages `mst.plugins.<id>`, entry points of the group `mst.plugins` and the directories of `MST_PLUGINS_PATH`. `python -m mst.plugin new --kind position|satellite|macro` creates a working package of any kind.
- **`mst.plugins.bvar`**: the BVAR with stochastic volatility moves to the plugin `bvar.sv`; the core `mst.macro` is the variable registry, the panel and the common version store. The shock table (the set $J$) belongs to the model (`signs.toml`); the seasonality of variables is a property of the data registry, not of the model specification.
- **`mst.plugins.hier` + bundle.** The hierarchical satellite is the plugin `hier.gibbs`; the core `mst.drivers` is the quantity registry, catalogs, `history` (data preparation) and `bundle`: a satellite version is assembled from parts, its own model per quantity, assigned by declaration and by an explicit `--model QUANTITY=MODEL` on ambiguity. Simulation dispatches drivers to their models with their impacts and their correlation slice.
- **Scenario.** `Overlay` → `Impact` with an address: a macro variable or a driver; an impact on level 0 applies before the satellites are simulated. The calculation writes the conditional forecast of target indicators `forecast.json` ($P_5/P_{50}/P_{95}$ by month); `run` gets `targets`.
- **`mst.metrics`**: a framework of quality metrics that checks models on historical episodes: cells $(e,t,q)$, CRPS normalized by $\sigma_q(t)$, the check $u=\hat F(y)$, sharpness, admission (value domain, historical range, probability of a condition in the base calculation), benchmarks (random walk with drift, naive annual), a bootstrap over episodes and the episode backtest `python -m mst.metrics backtest` with a cache of episode versions. Episodes are data: the config is in `mst/metrics/episodes.toml`. The BVAR-specific coverage backtest is removed.
- **MCP.** `model` gets `model_id`, `assignment` (quantity → satellite model) and `kind=catalog`; `backtest` goes to `mst.metrics`; `run` gets the parameter `targets`. The commands `python -m mst.macro` and `python -m mst.drivers estimate|check` are replaced by `python -m mst.models estimate-macro|estimate-satellites`.
- **Metrics: fixes.** The random walk benchmark for macro variables is no longer replaced by the model's base calculation (which is reported separately), $\sigma_q(t)$ and the historical range come from the history on the episode date and do not depend on `--only`, the bound of admissible values is on by default, the shares of violations of each admission rule are counted separately, the naive forecast shifts along the horizon, the report records versions, data, seed and commit, and the episode cache key includes the model specification. Acerbi–Székely, the cluster wild bootstrap and the split of episodes into halves remain next steps of the framework.

### Migration from 0.1.0

- **Saved models and scenarios.** A macro version without the field `model` is read by the only installed macro model, a BVAR specification with the removed `seasonal` without losing the other fields; a part of a satellite bundle without `model` and `quantity` goes to the only model that declared the quantity. The streams of satellite simulation are numbered from 1, so a bundle of one model reproduces saved calculations on the same seed; adding a second model changes the numbering (the bundle is rebuilt in that case anyway).
- **Scenarios.** The field `overlays` is renamed to `impacts`, the key `driver` inside it to `target`. Old files `scenarios/<id>.json` load without edits: while there is no `impacts`, `overlays` and `driver` are read as a deprecated alias, and a resaved scenario is written in the new format. A file with both fields is rejected with a conflict message; there is no silent choice between them.
- **Conditions and level-0 add-ons.** Conditions $C$ refer to the final path $F(\omega,O)$: the macro model solves them against a path shifted by the impact $o_0$ (the bounds fall by $w\Delta$), and the add-on is added to the paths after the draw. Previously a path could leave the bound of $C$ although `validate` answered "ok". The severity $\delta$ is still measured on $C$ without $O$.
- **Flags in results.** New calculations mark a driver shift `impact:<driver>`; calculations before this version carry `overlay:<driver>`, and both names need reading.
- **`forecast.json`.** A driver series no longer has the field `unit_months`: the horizon is the top-level `months`. Duplicates in `targets` of a request are rejected by name.

## Before 0.2.0

- **Scheme: amendments A1 and A3 from the analysis of fitting the regime kernel.** Uncertainty gained a path of discrete regimes: $\omega=(\zeta,\varepsilon,\eta,\theta)$, where $\zeta$ is drawn from a Markov kernel (a model without regimes keeps it constant, so the BVAR contract does not change), and $D$ addresses the regime elements $\zeta_D$ on a par with shocks. The scenario overlay is generalized to $P_s(d\omega)=P(d\theta,d\eta,d\zeta_{\bar D},d\varepsilon_{\bar D})\,Q_s(d\zeta_D,d\varepsilon_D\mid\theta,\eta,\zeta_{\bar D},\varepsilon_{\bar D})$: with regime-dependent parameters the law of the other shocks does not separate from the regime of the event.
- **`mst.plugins.msvar`: an MS-VAR with two regimes, the second macro model of level 0.** Gibbs (FFBS of the regime path, Minnesota priors by regime, Dirichlet on transitions, augmentation of missing months), sign identification of the same five shocks inside each regime, a conditional forecast along a fixed regime path by the common machinery `model_sdk.conditional`, severity and the moments of the mixture (the mean of covariances plus the covariance of means). The regime elements `regime.calm`/`regime.stress` enter the set $J$ of a version: naming one among the driving shocks fixes the regime of the event over the whole horizon. Regime labels are held by the covariance trace. Tests are in `tests/msvar` on simulated data from known parameters. With two installed macro models `estimate-macro` requires an explicit name.
- **Augmentation of missing MS-VAR months: equations by rows.** The equation operator was assembled without an equation number, so all $n$ equations of a month fell into one row of the matrix, and for missing values of the last months the columns coincided and the precision became degenerate; estimation failed on the Cholesky decomposition. The equation number is added, and the conditional mean of missing cells now comes from the same VAR equations; `tests/msvar/test_impute.py` checks it against a dense assembly of these equations and catches the original error.
- **Augmentation of missing MS-VAR months: error precision.** The precision was calculated as $A D^{-1} A'$ instead of $A' D^{-1} A$, so missing values were completed from a foreign distribution: on the working panel the residuals ran up to $10^7\sigma$, the stress regime collapsed to 5–6 months, and estimation failed on the Cholesky decomposition. After the fix, over 300 iterations on two seeds the residuals are no higher than $9.3\sigma$, with 53–59 months in the stress regime.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-29

### Added

- Series store with release dates and loading from MOEX ISS, the Bank of Russia, Rosstat, and FRED.
- Macro model: BVAR with stochastic volatility, sign restrictions, conditional forecasts, scenario severity, and checks on historical episodes.
- Drivers: object catalogs, hierarchical satellites, out-of-sample checks, and driver paths on the same draws as the macro forecast.
- Corpus of sources: manifest with hashes, downloads, Markdown conversion, episode cards, index, and search that respects the publication date.
- Scenario file $(N, C, D, O)$ with validation and paths.
- Asset models for equities, bonds, credit and deposits, and housing.
- Portfolio checks and a run over scenario paths with $P_5$, $P_{50}$, $P_{95}$, ES95, and $\delta$ in the report.
- Asset types as plugins: `python -m mst.plugin new|check|demo-run`, contract tests, and a registry that keeps `mst.engine` free of asset-specific branches.
- MCP server with the tools `jobs`, `series`, `corpus`, `scenario`, `model`, `portfolio`, and `run`.
- DeepSeek Harness plugin: three skills, the prompt section built from `workspace.json`, and Russian and English locales.
